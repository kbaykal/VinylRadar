Product Definition — v1.0

VinylRadar

A collector-grade, format-agnostic music library app. Free forever at the core. Honest about what's hard. Built to last beyond the incumbents.

Status Working PWA / pre-product
Target v1.0 Q3 2026
Platform PWA + responsive web

00

Vision & promise

Three sentences that must remain true on every decision we make.

The three commitments
  • You own your data. Full export, at any time, even without an account. If VinylRadar shuts down tomorrow, your collection leaves with you intact.
  • The core is free forever. Unlimited records. No scan limits. No feature gates on the things a collector fundamentally needs: add, find, grade, export.
  • It works where you are. Flea market, basement, no signal. Everything core works offline. Sync is transparent and never silent.

Who we're building for

The primary user is a collector with 50–2000 physical records (vinyl, CD, cassette) who manages their collection without a dedicated system today — spreadsheets, Discogs wishlists, memory. They are not a power-user audiophile. They are not a casual listener. They are the middle: they care deeply about what they own, they want to know what it's worth, and they're sick of every app either paywalling the basics or being abandonware.

A secondary user is the record store owner or serious trader — someone who annotates purchases, tracks acquisition price vs market value, and needs insurance documentation. We serve them with the same core, plus one well-designed paid add-on (the insurance PDF).

Design principle No feature ships if it would compromise the three commitments. When in doubt, build less and hold the line.
01

What we are not

Explicit scope cuts. These were evaluated and deliberately removed from v1 and v2 scope.

Feature Decision Reason
OpenVinylDB — proprietary community database Year 3+ Building a release database is a decade of work. We depend on Discogs + MusicBrainz. That's fine and honest. Revisit when user base can sustain community contributions.
Record store partnership / POS integration Removed This is a separate B2B product. It dilutes consumer focus and requires a different sales motion entirely. Cut permanently from v1–v3.
Social feed / "Spinning Now" friend activity Year 2+ Social cold-start problem is real. Build this only after a user base exists. Shareable shelf links (read-only, no account needed) serve the sharing use case in v1.
Matrix number OCR (runout groove reading) v2 / beta OCR on etched vinyl under variable lighting fails 50–70% of the time in real conditions. Ship it only as an explicitly-labelled beta, with manual entry always available as the primary fallback.
Family sharing (up to 5 accounts) Removed Nobody asks for this in a vinyl tracker. "Share a collection view" (read-only link) is what people actually want and it's free.
Native iOS / Android apps Year 2+ The existing PWA is solid. PWA covers scanning (BarcodeDetector API), offline, home-screen install, push notifications. Native is an investment for when the PWA is feature-complete and has validated user retention.
Affiliate marketplace links v2, limited Permissible only as a single discreet "find this on Discogs / eBay" deep-link on the record detail page. No pop-ups, no price-comparison widgets, no affiliate-first UX. Revenue ceiling is low; don't design around it.

Core data model

Every item in VinylRadar is a Record. Every field listed below is a first-class, queryable, exportable field — not a note buried in free text.

Record — core fields

Field Type Description
iduuidClient-generated, stable across devices. Never server-assigned.
formatenumVinyl · CD · Cassette · 8-Track · Reel-to-Reel · Lathe Cut · Digital · Other. Required field, not defaulting to vinyl.
artiststringPrimary artist name. Searchable, sortable.
titlestringAlbum or release title.
yearintegerRelease year of this pressing (not original recording year). Separate original_year field if known.
labelstringRecord label name. Affects pressing identification significantly.
catalog_numberstringLabel catalog number. Key pressing identifier for pre-barcode records.
countrystringCountry of pressing. Affects both collectibility and grading norms.
barcodestringEAN/UPC barcode as scanned. Stored as string to preserve leading zeros.
discogs_idstringDiscogs release ID for deep-link and re-fetch. Nullable.
musicbrainz_idstringMusicBrainz release MBID. Nullable. Stored alongside Discogs ID; not merged.
genrestring[ ]Array of genre tags. User-editable in addition to what APIs return.
media_conditionenumM · NM · VG+ · VG · G+ · G · P using Goldmine standard. Stored as string key, displayed as label.
sleeve_conditionenumSame scale. Tracked independently from media.
condition_notesstringFree text — "light hairlines visible under raking light", "sleeve split bottom seam". Not merged into condition field.
purchase_pricedecimalWhat you paid. In user-selected currency. Used in value delta calculations.
purchase_datedateISO date of acquisition.
purchase_sourcestringFree text — "Boomkat", "Record Fair Den Bosch", "gift from Joren". Not forced into a dropdown.
locationstringWhere it's physically stored. "Shelf A, crate 3", "Geldrop studio", "lent to Emre". Free text, but queryable.
for_salebooleanFlags a record for the personal "for sale" view. No marketplace integration in v1.
wishlistbooleanItem is wanted, not yet owned. Wishlist and collection share the same Record model; the wishlist is a filter view, not a separate object.
tracklistTrack[ ]Array of {position, title, duration}. Populated from API on scan; user-editable.
cover_image_urlstringRemote URL from Discogs/Cover Art Archive. Never stored locally unless user explicitly saves for offline viewing (v2).
pressing_notesstringUser notes about this specific pressing — "original UK Harvest pressing", "DMM cut", "Quiex SV-P vinyl". This is the most underserved field in competitors.
created_attimestampWhen added to collection.
updated_attimestampLast local modification. Used for CRDT merge ordering.
tagsstring[ ]User-defined custom tags. "lent out", "needs cleaning", "birthday gift". Freeform, queryable.
Key design decision Purchase data (price, date, source) and storage location are first-class fields, not buried in notes. These are the two fields competitors universally omit that serious collectors actually need.

Market data — separate object, not baked in

Market valuation is fetched on demand and stored as a separate MarketSnapshot object linked to a record by ID. It is never conflated with condition-adjusted estimated value. The user always sees both separately.

MarketSnapshot fields
  • discogs_median_eur — median of last 90 days sold on Discogs Marketplace
  • discogs_low / discogs_high — current listing range (not sold)
  • discogs_sales_count — number of data points the median is based on (shown to user)
  • fetched_at — timestamp. Shown as "as of [date]" in UI. Never hidden.
  • currency — EUR default, user-settable per collection
Popsike / eBay completed auctions Popsike is paywalled and scraping violates ToS. eBay's completed-auction API requires Partner Network approval and has restrictive access. These are not v1 data sources. If they become available legitimately, they are added as supplemental sources with their own fetched_at and labeled accordingly in the UI. We never silently blend data sources.
04

Scanning engine

The single most important UX moment in the app. Every second of friction here costs a scan.

Barcode scan — primary path

The scan button is the centerpiece of the app. It opens the camera directly — no intermediate screen. The app uses the browser-native BarcodeDetector API where available (Chromium 83+, Safari 17.4+). For unsupported browsers, a polyfill library is bundled. For users who cannot or will not camera-scan, a manual barcode entry field is always one tap below the camera view.

Scan flow — every state must be handled
  • Barcode detected → spinner → result card slides up within 2 seconds on a good connection
  • Result found with high confidence (Discogs match, barcode unique) → show record card, one-tap to add
  • Multiple matches found → show a swipeable disambiguation row: "Found 3 pressings for this barcode — pick yours"
  • Low confidence / single API hit → show result with amber badge, offer "View all Discogs results" link
  • No match found → immediately surface "Add manually" with any clues we have (barcode stored, label from cover if user provides photo). Never a blank form.
  • Offline → lookup attempted against local cache. If barcode was previously scanned on any device in this account, show cached result. If not, prompt manual entry with note that we'll enrich automatically when back online.

Source priority and merge logic

We query Discogs first (with user's token if set, public API otherwise) and MusicBrainz as a fallback. We do not query both simultaneously and try to merge them — conflicting metadata between Discogs and MusicBrainz is a real, common problem and silently merging it produces wrong data.

SourceUsed forRequiresLimitation
Discogs (authenticated)Primary: all fields, market dataUser token (free)Rate limited: 60 req/min authenticated
Discogs (public)Primary fallback: metadata only, no market dataNothing25 req/min, no price suggestions
MusicBrainz + Cover Art ArchiveFallback: metadata, cover art, classical/obscureNothing1 req/sec rate limit — must queue, not parallel

Confidence badge — honest definition

The confidence badge reflects one specific question: "How certain are we that this is the correct pressing?"

Confidence tiers
  • High (green) — barcode matched exactly one Discogs release. No disambiguation needed.
  • Medium (amber) — barcode matched multiple releases, user selected one; OR barcode matched but pressing details are sparse. Show badge, let user review.
  • Low (red) — no barcode match; result is a best-guess from artist+title search, or manually entered. Badge persists until user confirms record details.
  • No badge — manually added and user has confirmed all core fields. Record is considered "verified".

Duplicate detection

Before adding any scanned record, we check: does this barcode, or this discogs_id, already exist in the user's collection? If yes, we surface a "You already own this" notice with the existing entry shown. The user can still add it (some collectors own multiples) but we don't silently duplicate.

Manual entry — never a dead end

Manual add is never a blank form. We pre-fill whatever we have — barcode string, any text from a user-provided photo if OCR is available. The form fields are ordered by importance (format → artist → title → year → label) not alphabetically. All fields except format, artist, and title are optional. A record with just those three fields is a complete, valid entry.

05

Collection management

The daily-driver view. Most users will spend more time here than anywhere else.

Views and navigation

Three top-level tabs: Collection, Wishlist, Stats. The scan button is a persistent floating button, always visible in Collection and Wishlist views — it never goes away. Settings are behind a gear icon, not in the main navigation.

Filtering and search

Required filter and search capabilities — v1
  • Free-text search across artist, title, label, notes, pressing_notes, tags
  • Filter by format (multi-select)
  • Filter by genre (multi-select)
  • Filter by condition (media_condition range — e.g. VG+ and above)
  • Filter by decade (1960s, 1970s, etc.)
  • Filter by country
  • Filter by for_sale flag
  • Filter by custom tag
  • Sort by: artist A–Z, title A–Z, year asc/desc, date added, estimated value desc, purchase price
  • Combined: filters + sort persist within a session. Shareable as a URL parameter for the web companion.

Bulk operations

Select multiple records (long-press on mobile, checkbox on web) and apply: change genre tag, change custom tag, add to a sub-collection, mark as for-sale, export selected, delete. Bulk edit respects undo — one tap reverses the last bulk action.

Sub-collections

Users can create named sub-collections (e.g. "Jazz shelf", "In storage in Geldrop", "For sale"). A record can belong to multiple sub-collections. Sub-collections are views over the main collection, not separate data silos. Deleting a sub-collection does not delete the records in it.

Wishlist

Wishlisted items use the same Record model with wishlist: true. Adding a wishlisted item to your collection flips the flag and prompts for purchase_price and purchase_date — it doesn't create a duplicate. A wishlist item can be shared via a read-only link for birthday/gift purposes without exposing the full collection.

Shareable shelf link

Any collection or sub-collection can generate a public, read-only URL. The recipient sees cover art, artist, title, format badge, and year. They do not see condition, purchase price, or location. No account required to view. The link can be revoked at any time. This is the entire "social" feature set for v1.

Missing from competitors, present here Location field, pressing_notes field, duplicate detection on scan, purchase source tracking, and custom tags are the five fields serious collectors want and no existing app gives cleanly.
06

Valuation engine

We show you what your records are worth. We are transparent about how we calculated it. We never hide the data source or the age of the data.

Condition-adjusted value — the calculation

Raw market data (Discogs median sold price) is adjusted for the condition of the specific copy the user owns. The formula uses a Goldmine multiplier table with a 70/30 blend between media and sleeve condition.

Goldmine condition multipliers
  • M (Mint): 1.40 × median — rarely traded, commands premium
  • NM (Near Mint): 1.00 × median — baseline for most Discogs pricing
  • VG+ (Very Good Plus): 0.70 × median
  • VG (Very Good): 0.40 × median
  • G+ (Good Plus): 0.15 × median
  • G (Good): 0.05 × median
  • P (Poor): not appraised — shown as "worn / no value estimate"

The displayed estimate = (media_mult × 0.7 + sleeve_mult × 0.3) × discogs_median. This formula is shown to the user on demand — tapping "How is this calculated?" opens a plain-language explanation of the math.

What we show on each record

Valuation display — required elements
  • Condition-adjusted estimate with low–high range
  • Raw Discogs median (labeled: "Discogs median, NM condition")
  • Number of sales this median is based on ("Based on 47 sales, last 90 days")
  • Data freshness ("Fetched 3 days ago") — tappable to refresh
  • Purchase price vs estimate delta ("+€12 since you bought it" or "−€4")
  • Confidence warning if sales count is below 5: "Fewer than 5 recent sales — estimate may not reflect true market value"

Collection-level stats

The Stats tab shows: total estimated value, total purchase cost, overall delta (profit/loss on paper), value by format, value by genre, value by decade, top-10 most valuable records. These are calculated locally from cached market snapshots — no additional API calls on load.

Insurance report — the one paid-tier feature that actually converts

From the Stats view: "Generate Insurance Report" button (Plus feature). Produces a PDF containing:

Insurance report — PDF contents
  • User name, report date, unique report ID (UUID shown on cover page)
  • One row per record: cover thumbnail, artist, title, format, label, year, media condition, sleeve condition, condition notes, estimated replacement value
  • Total collection replacement value (sum of estimated values)
  • Data sources and methodology footnote (Discogs Marketplace pricing, Goldmine grading scale, calculation method)
  • SHA-256 hash of the report contents — allows insurer or user to verify the document hasn't been altered
  • No claim about insurer acceptance is made in the UI — we describe it as "a documentation record suitable for insurance conversations" not "accepted by Chubb/AXA"
Important constraint Do not claim in any marketing or in-app copy that insurers accept this document. Insurer acceptance varies and claiming it without verification is misleading. The report's value is in being a timestamped, tamper-evident inventory — that's enough.
07

Offline & sync

This is the hardest engineering section and the one most specs hand-wave. We don't.

Storage architecture

All record data lives in IndexedDB on the client. This is the primary store — the cloud is a sync target, not the source of truth. Everything the user does writes locally first. Network state is irrelevant to core operations.

Sync strategy — what we actually build, not what sounds good

Honest scope Full CRDT sync is the right long-term architecture but it is also a significant engineering investment with non-obvious edge cases. For v1, we implement a simpler "last-write-wins with conflict notification" strategy. CRDT is a v2 upgrade — after we've seen real sync failures and understand the actual conflict patterns in this data.
v1 sync approach
  • Every record has updated_at timestamp. On sync, we compare timestamps between client and server.
  • Client-wins for all mutations that originated offline (user was clearly working locally)
  • If the same record was edited on two devices while both were online (simultaneous edit), we surface a diff notification: "This record was updated on two devices. Tap to review." User picks which version to keep.
  • Sync state is always visible: cloud icon in nav bar with three states (synced, syncing, offline-changes-pending). Tapping it shows a log of pending operations.
  • Sync happens on app foreground after offline period, on explicit user pull-to-refresh, and on a 5-minute interval when online.

The no-account experience

Without an account, all data lives in IndexedDB. The user is prompted once — gently, non-blocking — to create a free account to enable sync and backup. They can use the app indefinitely without one. Without an account, they can still manually export JSON at any time. If they clear browser data, their collection is gone — we tell them this clearly in the onboarding prompt, once, without alarming them.

PWA service worker

A service worker pre-caches the app shell and all static assets. The app loads and is fully interactive with zero network. Market data fetches fail gracefully offline — showing cached values with a "fetched X days ago" note rather than empty states or errors.

08

Data freedom

Export formats — all free, all complete
  • JSON — full data model including all fields, suitable for re-import to VinylRadar or custom processing
  • CSV — flat format with all non-array fields, array fields comma-joined in one column. Compatible with Excel, Google Sheets, Airtable.
  • Discogs import-compatible CSV — a specific column mapping that matches Discogs' bulk import format, allowing migration back to Discogs collection if the user wants
Backup targets — v2, after cloud sync is stable
  • Google Drive — OAuth, writes to a VinylRadar folder, one file per export
  • iCloud Drive (iOS PWA) — writes to Files app via Share Sheet
  • Manual download — always available, no authentication required
The Discogs-compatible export is a real differentiator No collector should feel locked in. Providing a Discogs-import-compatible CSV removes the last psychological barrier to trying VinylRadar — they know they can always go back.

Free & paid tiers

The free tier is the product. Plus is an honest upgrade, not a gate.

Free — forever €0 / always
  • Unlimited records across all formats
  • Unlimited collections and sub-collections
  • Full barcode scanning (BarcodeDetector + manual fallback)
  • Discogs + MusicBrainz metadata lookup
  • Goldmine condition grading
  • Condition-adjusted valuation with Discogs market data
  • Purchase price tracking and value delta
  • Wishlist
  • All data fields — location, pressing notes, custom tags, purchase source
  • Full CSV and JSON export at any time
  • Shareable read-only shelf link
  • Offline-first: all core operations work with no signal
  • Web companion at app.vinylradar.com
  • Cloud sync (requires free account)
  • Duplicate detection on scan
No scan limits. No item caps. No time trials. No "you've reached your free limit" popups. Ever.
VinylRadar Plus €2.99 / month · €24.99 / year
  • Insurance report PDF — timestamped, SHA-256 signed inventory with estimated replacement values. The most concrete, tangible paid feature.
  • Analytics dashboard — collection growth over time, value trend chart, genre/decade/format distribution, acquisition spending by year, most-appreciated records over time
  • Price alert — watch a wishlist item and get notified when a new Discogs listing appears below a target price
  • Listening journal — log plays with a date stamp and optional note. View play history per record. Lifetime play counts. This is the only "listening" feature and it's manual — no scrobbling integration in v1.
  • Scheduled cloud backup — weekly automatic JSON export to Google Drive or iCloud
  • Early access to new features before general release
Plus does not unlock data — it unlocks tools. A free user's collection is identical in data richness to a Plus user's. Plus adds analysis, documentation, and automation on top of that data.
Why the original Plus tier fails The spec's original Plus list included "ad-free" (there are no ads in the free tier — contradictory), "family sharing" (no one asks for this), "high-res cover art" (already free on Cover Art Archive), and "custom themes" (not a $3/month value prop for collectors). The revised tier above leads with the insurance report and analytics — the two features that are genuinely worth paying for.
10

Revenue model

Primary: Plus subscriptions

At €24.99/year, we need roughly 400 paying users to cover basic infrastructure costs (hosting, API costs, Stripe fees) for a solo-operated product. At 2,000 paying users, it pays a meaningful part-time wage. The conversion target is 5–8% of registered accounts, consistent with well-run niche utilities. The insurance report PDF is the main conversion driver — it's a feature with a clear, concrete benefit that costs nothing to explain.

Secondary: affiliate deep-links

On each record detail page, a single "Find this on Discogs" and "Find this on eBay" link — deep-linked to the specific release, not a generic search. These use affiliate links where programs exist. Revenue ceiling is low (Discogs has no affiliate program as of 2026; eBay Partner Network pays ~1–4% on used items). This is a supplementary income line, not a business model. We never design UX around it.

What we explicitly do not do

Revenue anti-patterns — ruled out
  • No advertising — ever. No ad slots, no sponsored placements, no "promoted" records in search results.
  • No data selling — collection data is personal and private. It is never aggregated and sold to record labels, market research firms, or anyone else.
  • No "your data is held hostage" — free users export their full data at any time. Cancelling Plus doesn't delete records; it removes Plus-feature access.
  • No freemium bait-and-switch — features in the free tier today stay free. The free tier may grow; it will never shrink.

Database strategy

The honest version: we depend on Discogs and MusicBrainz. That dependency is managed, not denied.

Current dependency map

FunctionProviderRiskMitigation
Barcode → release lookup Discogs API Medium — API could restrict/paywall MusicBrainz fallback; user's own token for auth'd tier
Classical / obscure / international MusicBrainz Low — open and community-run No mitigation needed; MusicBrainz is stable
Cover art Discogs images / Cover Art Archive Low Cover Art Archive is MusicBrainz-adjacent and stable
Market pricing Discogs Marketplace API High — Discogs monetizes this data We cache pricing per record; re-fetch is optional, not required. If Discogs removes pricing API, users keep their cached data and we surface a notice.

Community corrections — not a database, a layer

Users can flag incorrect metadata on a record ("This is wrong on Discogs — the catalog number should be ILPS 9016"). These corrections are stored in VinylRadar's own database as user-local overrides. They do not attempt to build a parallel release database — they patch the user's own view of their record. In aggregate, if we see the same correction flagged by 10+ users for the same Discogs release ID, we surface it as a community-suggested correction — but we do not attempt to maintain a release database independent of Discogs until the product has the user base to sustain it.

Long-term goal — Year 3+ If we reach 50,000+ active users with meaningful engagement, building a community-maintained pressing database becomes viable. Until then, the "OpenVinylDB" concept is aspirational, not in-scope. Naming it in the product spec as a current feature is a credibility problem we avoid.
12

Technical stack

We build on what exists. The current working PWA is the foundation. No rewrites.

v1 stack — production
  • Single-file PWA (HTML/CSS/JS) — deployable to GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages with zero build step. This is the existing architecture; we don't migrate away from it without a specific reason.
  • BarcodeDetector API for scanning, with ZXing.js polyfill for unsupported browsers
  • IndexedDB via the Dexie.js wrapper — structured queries, migrations, typed schema
  • Service worker via Workbox — pre-caches app shell, handles offline fallback for API requests
  • Discogs API + MusicBrainz REST API for metadata
  • jsPDF for insurance report generation (client-side, no server needed)
  • Supabase free tier for cloud sync — PostgreSQL backend, row-level security, Realtime for sync events. No proprietary lock-in; the schema is portable to any Postgres host.
  • Stripe for subscription payments
Web companion — same codebase, responsive
  • Not a separate app. The same index.html, responsive to desktop viewport.
  • Desktop adds: keyboard navigation, bulk-edit checkboxes, wider record grid, advanced search with operator syntax (year:1973, genre:jazz, condition:nm)
  • Drag-and-drop cover image upload on desktop only
  • All data sync is identical; no feature disparity in core features
Why no native app in v1 The existing PWA covers the core use cases: camera scanning, offline, home-screen install, push notifications (where browser supports it). Native adds significant maintenance overhead — two codebases, App Store review cycles, Apple developer fee, Google Play fee. The right trigger for going native is: user retention is strong, organic growth is real, and there is a specific capability gap (e.g. background sync, widget) that PWA cannot address.

Risk register

What can kill this product, and what we do about it.

RiskLikelihoodImpactMitigation
Discogs restricts or paywalls their API Medium High MusicBrainz fallback for metadata. Cached pricing data survives. Prompt users to grab Discogs token now while free. Build export-first so users aren't stranded.
Plus tier doesn't convert — too few paying users Medium Medium Infrastructure cost is low on Supabase free + Cloudflare Pages. The app survives at low conversion. Revisit Plus feature mix after 6 months of data.
Supabase free tier limits hit (500MB, 50k rows) Low (initially) → High (growth) Medium Supabase Pro is $25/month — absorbed by ~10 Plus subscribers. Plan migration path in advance; schema is portable Postgres.
BarcodeDetector API removed or restricted in browsers Low Medium ZXing.js polyfill already bundled as fallback. This is a belt-and-suspenders situation already.
Discogs releases an official PWA / improves their mobile app Medium Medium Our differentiators (unlimited free, format-agnostic, full export, purchase tracking, offline-first) are structural. They would require Discogs to fundamentally change their business model to match.
Solo developer burnout / project abandonment Real High The three commitments include data freedom specifically because of this risk. If the project stops, users export their data. The phased roadmap is intentionally scoped to sustainable solo output. No promises beyond Phase 2 are made publicly until Phase 1 is shipped.
